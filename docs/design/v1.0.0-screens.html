bitlaForge, screen by screen

Every screen of the new bitlaForge, drawn before any code is written. Javier approved all six answers below on 2 October.

Your brief: "rework alacrittyForge and bitlaForge to match the look of grubForge, so far the best of the 3." bitlaForge is different from its sisters: it runs a program for hours (the miner) instead of editing a file. So it keeps a live Dashboard, and the grubForge look comes in through the Settings form, the changes bar, the hint line, a review before every save, and the manual inside the app. Every drawing is exactly 100 columns wide. The numbers in them are today's real ones, checked on this computer at 22:55.

Decided · Javier, 2 October

  1. Version 1.0.0, like alacrittyForge: a rebuilt app with tests, not a step from 0.2.1. "Yes"
  2. Four screens: Dashboard, Settings, Log, and a new History that keeps every mining session between runs (today, all the numbers vanish the moment you stop). "Love it!"
  3. Bitcoin only. The algorithm list shrinks from eight to one, sha256d. Six of today's eight can't run on the miner bitlaForge recommends, and this is a Bitcoin app. "Bitcoin"
  4. Pause when hot, on by default at 85 °C: bitlaForge pauses the miner when the processor reaches it and resumes 5 °C lower. Today nothing watches the heat. "Ok"
  5. The lottery odds, honestly: once an hour bitlaForge asks mempool.space how big the Bitcoin network is and shows your real chance. It sends nothing about you. A switch turns it off, and then no internet is used for this. "Wow! I like it!"
  6. Closing bitlaForge always stops the miner, after asking. Today it keeps mining with nothing on screen (checked below). "Sounds good!"

What today's version gets wrong

I took stock of bitlaForge 0.2.1 first and checked the serious findings on this computer, with a stand-in for the miner so nothing was mined.

Found and checked
  • Quitting leaves the miner running. I started a stand-in miner from the app and pressed Q: the app closed, the miner kept going. On a real run your processor would keep working at full load with nothing on screen to stop it.
  • Rejected shares are never counted. The miner reports a rejection as part of its "accepted: 37/38 (booooo)" line, and bitlaForge only looks for the word "rejected". It always shows 0.
  • The pool field's example is wrong. It says "host:port" works. Without stratum+tcp:// in front, the miner treats the address as a web address and never connects.
  • A broken settings file is replaced silently. If it can't be read, bitlaForge starts from blank settings without a word, and the next Save overwrites it. No backup is kept.
Typed by hand

Pool address, wallet, thread count and priority, with no checks: "abc" threads or a mistyped wallet go straight to the miner.

Arch only, and not nog

The help says yay -S cpuminer and nothing about other distributions.

Out of date

No tests, no --version/--help, the old name "BitlaForge" on screen, colours and marks that don't show on a text console, Ctrl+C taken over, and the Log's "Auto-scroll" switch does nothing.

Then the terminal gets the closing note every Forge app gives: what ran, for how long, the shares, where the run was logged, and a thank-you.

Keys

The family's keys: 1–4 screens, Tab moves, F10 saves, F1 help, M start/stop mining, T test the miner, ? all keys, Q quit. Ctrl+C goes back to meaning "copy". The manual (in the Help menu) covers pools, wallets, the lottery maths, heat and every setting.

How it gets built

  1. The safe core first, with tests: settings saved like alacrittyForge's (review, backup, one-step write, never over a broken file), the wallet check, the miner's start and stop (stops on quit), and reading its output correctly, rejected shares included.
  2. The screens, on forgekit 0.5.1, readable on a text console and at 100 columns.
  3. Heat, odds and History.
  4. Every distribution: the miner is built in VMs of Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora and openSUSE, the way alacrittyForge's Alacritty was.
  5. Your run, with a written test matrix, including real mining on this computer. Then the release.
